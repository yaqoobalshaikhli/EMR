# AI-writing patterns to find and fix

Source for most of this list: Wikipedia, "Signs of AI writing" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing), a volunteer-maintained catalogue. Only the first part of that page was read when this file was written, so treat the list as incomplete. The page itself says specific word lists shift as models change, so use the categories as the durable part and the example words as a starting point.

None of these is proof on its own. One "crucial" in a text means nothing. A cluster of several is the signal. Do not rewrite a word that is the right word.

## 1. Vocabulary

Words the source lists as overused in model output (by period of use):
- Earlier: additionally, boasts, bolstered, crucial, delve, emphasizing, enduring, garner, intricate/intricacies, interplay, landscape, meticulously, pivotal, underscore, tapestry, testament, valuable, vibrant
- Later: align with, enhance, fostering, highlighting, showcasing
- Most recent entries on the page: challenge, clearer, dependable, echoed, foster, leverage, matters, multifaceted, practical, prioritize, quietly, steady, universally

Fix: use the plain word the writer would speak. "Crucial" becomes "important" or, better, say why it matters. "Delve into" becomes "look at". "Leverage" becomes "use".

Promotional words: boasts a, nestled, in the heart of, groundbreaking, renowned, natural beauty, commitment to, diverse array.
Fix: state the fact ("has 40 rooms") and drop the sales language.

## 2. Phrasing

| Pattern | Example | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Copula avoidance | "serves as", "stands as", "functions as", "features", "offers" | "is", "has" |
| Significance inflation | "is a testament to", "marks a key turning point", "leaves an indelible mark", "deeply rooted" | Delete, or state the concrete consequence |
| Superficial -ing tack-ons | "..., highlighting its importance", "..., reflecting broader trends", "..., ensuring quality" | Cut the clause, or make it its own sentence with a real claim |
| Negative parallelism | "It's not just X, it's Y", "Not only X but also Y" | Say Y directly |
| Reversed contrast | "Y rather than X" used as a reflex | Keep only if the contrast is real |
| Challenge formula | "Despite these challenges, ..." | Name the actual challenge and what happened, or cut |
| Vague attribution | "Experts argue", "Industry reports suggest", "Observers have noted" | Name the source, or cut the claim. Never make a source up |
| Vague connection | "associated with", "in connection with" | Say what the relationship is |

## 3. Structure

- Everything in threes ("fast, reliable, and scalable"). Keep a triple only if there really are three things.
- Headings in the form "X and Y" ("Awards and Recognition") and stock closing sections ("Future Outlook", "Challenges and Legacy"). Replace with a heading that says what the section holds, or remove it.
- A closing paragraph that restates the whole piece and ends on vague positivity. End where the content ends.
- Opening that restates the question or announces what the text will do ("In this essay, we will explore...").
- Uniform paragraph length and a template feel: every paragraph a topic sentence, three supports, a wrap-up.

## 4. Formatting

- Heavy bold, bold lead-ins on every bullet, emoji bullets, and markdown headings in text that would normally be plain prose (emails, posts, letters).
- Bullet lists where a sentence or two would do.
- Leftover placeholders, broken markup, tracking strings such as `utm_source=` in links.

## 5. Tone

- Press-release or brochure voice about ordinary subjects.
- Overemphasis on legacy, significance and "broader trends".
- Editorializing about nuance ("it's important to note", "it's worth noting that").
- Claims that a subject is notable, backed only by a list of places that mentioned it.
- Relentless balance: every point softened with a counterpoint.

Fix: take a position the content supports, or say plainly that it is uncertain and why.

## 6. Punctuation and rhythm

- Heavy em dash use. Replace most with a comma, period, colon or parentheses. Leave one where it really is the best mark.
- A steady drumbeat of similar-length sentences. Mix long and short because the thought calls for it, not by formula.
- Over-correcting is its own tell: do not swap every em dash for a fragment, and do not pile on contractions, rhetorical questions or "honestly" and "look" openers.

## 7. Chatbot leftovers

Remove: "Here is a rewritten version", "I hope this helps", "As an AI", "Certainly!", "Let me know if you'd like...", unfinished sentences at the end, and canned assurances about what was preserved or avoided.

## How confident is this list?

The categories are well documented by editors who review a lot of model text. The specific words are time-bound and will drift. Nothing here has been validated against AI detectors, and no rewrite can be guaranteed to pass one.
