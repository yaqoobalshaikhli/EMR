The film was 45.8 s and the words are written on the screen, so the voice does not
have to carry the information. It carries the **feeling**. Read slowly, warm and
close, like someone telling a story to a person sitting next to them: not an
announcer, not a salesman. Pause where there is a "..."

Record each line **as its own take** (two takes each): then each one can be placed
exactly on the picture. The time is when the line should *start*; the pen on the
screen takes about a second to write it, so begin a hair after the pen.

## How to record it (a phone is enough)

1. **A quiet, soft room.** A bedroom with the door closed and a blanket or pillows
   around you, or inside a parked car, beats any big room: echo is what ruins a
   voice. Fans, air conditioning and the fridge off.
2. **The phone's own recorder.** iPhone: *Voice Memos*. Android: *Recorder*. Hold
   the phone about a hand's width (15–20 cm) from your mouth, slightly to the side,
   not straight in front (that avoids the puff on «ب» and «پ»). Speak at normal
   loudness; do not shout.
3. **One line, one take, a second of silence before and after.** Two takes of each
   line. Say the number of the line at the start of each take («واحد»، «اثنين»...):
   it makes sorting easy.
4. **Headphones on while you read from the screen**, if you can watch the film
   while you record: it keeps your pace right. Not required.
5. **Send me the audio files** (m4a, mp3, wav or aac are all fine), in order or
   named by number. If they are all in one long recording, that works too: I will
   cut it by the numbers.
6. I will place each line on its picture, lower the music under your voice, clean a
   little noise, bring it to the same loudness as the music, and render the final film.

## If you prefer to edit it yourself in CapCut

Yes, it works: import the MP4, tap *Audio → Voiceover*, record on the timeline while
the picture plays, and export at 1080p. Two things to know: the film already has a
music score in it, so your voice will sit on top of it at its normal level (turn the
film's volume down to about 40 % in CapCut); and I cannot open CapCut myself, so any
timing fix would come back to me as a new file. Descript, DaVinci Resolve and even
the phone's own gallery editor can do the same job; CapCut is the easiest.
